# What MemoryFence accomplished

MemoryFence is a working research library and CLI for studying how untrusted text
moves through persistent agent memory. It separates the provenance available to a
defense from the true ancestry available only to an evaluator.

In three synthetic attack chains, source-only deletion left both generated
descendants. Cascade deletion removed all six poisoned descendants when recorded
ancestry was complete. Missing ancestry left four or six descendants alive and
also prevented the tested retrieval filter from recognizing their peer origin.

That persistence did not become the intended tool attack in the final bounded
behavioral study: all 36 calls completed legitimate tickets and 0/18 attack
episodes produced the attacker objective. Three free-text fields still echoed
poisoned instructions. One clean transformation lost useful information.

A separate 278-configuration abstract graph exercise shows why chain depth and
missing-edge position change cascade reach. It adds no model evidence.

The deliverable is a reproducible bounded research artifact and a compiled academic
manuscript, complete with limitations. It is not a production security product,
a general defense guarantee, or a published or peer-reviewed paper. Human semantic
validation, broad held-out evaluation and richer memory-dependent tasks remain
future research. Public visibility does not supply the still-unselected license.
